The problem

The Internet Connects Us. But Not Everything We See Is Healthy.

Teenagers spend hours a day in feeds they did not design. Alongside everything useful, those feeds carry graphic imagery, harassment, unrealistic messaging about bodies and appearance, and material related to self-harm — usually with no warning and no way to opt out of one post without leaving the whole platform.

MindShield AI grew out of two surveys conducted in a middle school, which the student developer designed and ran before writing any code.

Study 1 · Smartphone use

Smartphone Application-Based Addiction Scale (SABAS)

148 middle school students surveyed.

41%identified as being at moderate-to-severe risk for problematic smartphone use
148students surveyed

Higher risk was associated with compulsive phone checking, increased smartphone use, reduced physical activity, and smartphone-related symptoms.

Study 2 · Adolescent mental health

Patient Health Questionnaire-9 (PHQ-9)

147 middle school students surveyed.

50.3%reported at least mild depressive symptoms
13.6%scored in the moderately severe or severe range
19.3%reported some level of thoughts related to self-harm or suicide

Scientific note. These observational studies did not establish that online content caused the reported mental health outcomes. They describe what students reported at one point in time, and they motivated the project — they do not prove that filtering content changes these measures.

The solution

A Smarter Way to Experience the Internet.

Traditional parental controls work at the level of a whole website: a domain is either allowed or blocked. MindShield AI works at the level of individual content. A page stays open; the specific image or message a user chose to filter is blurred or hidden, and everything else stays exactly as it was.

Violence & disturbing imagery

Identifies potentially graphic, violent, or frightening images on supported pages.

Sexual & explicit content

Detects potentially explicit visual material before it is displayed in full.

Cyberbullying

Identifies potentially harmful messages, insults, and harassment in page text.

Self-harm content

Detects selected language associated with self-harm and related harmful messaging.

Body-image messaging

Identifies potentially harmful messages about appearance, body image, and unrealistic expectations.

You decide what applies

Each category is a switch. Turn on the ones that matter to you, leave the rest off, and change your mind whenever you want. Filtering is a preference, not a punishment.

How it works

Three steps, and then it stays out of the way.

1

Choose your filters

Open the extension and pick the content categories you want filtered. Nothing is switched on for you by default except the ones you select.

2

Browse normally

MindShield AI analyzes supported images and text on pages as you browse. You do not change how you use the internet.

3

Stay in control

Content matching your selected categories is blurred or hidden. You can still choose to view it, and you can switch filtering off at any time.

Webpage content→ AI analysis→ Your preferences→ Content filtering

How the prototype runs today. A Chrome extension reads supported page content and passes it to a Python AI backend running on the same computer. Image and text analysis work in the current prototype; video-frame analysis is experimental and not reliable yet.

Privacy & personalization

Your Browsing. Your Preferences.

MindShield AI is built around the idea that the person browsing should be the person deciding. Both AI models run on the user's own computer, so the pages being analyzed are never sent anywhere.

You pick the categories

Filtering applies only to the categories you select. Everything else is left untouched.

You can turn it off

Protection can be disabled at any time, for a session or entirely, without uninstalling anything.

Analysis runs on your machine

Both models load into a Python backend on your own computer. There is no API key, no account, and no third-party service in the loop.

A dashboard, not a log of you

The dashboard summarizes filtering activity. The prototype is designed to avoid intentionally storing original browsing content or detailed browsing histories.

What this does not claim. MindShield AI is an experimental prototype. It does not claim complete anonymity or absolute privacy, and its design has not been independently audited.

The story

From Research to Real-World Innovation.

MindShield AI was created by a teenage student researcher who ran two surveys on adolescent smartphone use and mental health in a middle school. The findings did not point toward telling teenagers to use their phones less — they pointed toward giving them more control over what those phones show them.

“What if we could use artificial intelligence to make the internet healthier instead of simply telling teenagers to use it less?”

The project was developed for the Congressional App Challenge.

The technology

Built with Artificial Intelligence.

The Chrome extension is JavaScript, HTML and CSS. It watches a page as it loads and as new content appears, then passes supported images and text to a Python AI backend running on the same computer. Two models do the classifying, and both of them run on that machine.

CLIP for images and video frames

Pictures go to CLIP, a vision-language model. It compares each image against short text descriptions of the categories the user has switched on — zero-shot classification, so it needs no training examples of its own.

Natural language classification for text

Page text is run through natural language classification to pick up cyberbullying, harassment, and self-harm-related messaging before it is displayed.

Everything runs locally

Both models load into the Python backend on the user’s own computer. Nothing is sent to an outside service, which is why no API key or account is needed to run it.

What it is built from

Chrome Extension APIs JavaScript HTML & CSS Python backend CLIP (vision-language model) Natural language classification Video-frame analysis (experimental) Customizable filtering controls

Problems worth solving

Building it meant working through browser permissions, content that loads after a page is already open, capturing frames out of video players, keeping analysis fast enough not to interrupt browsing, and getting the extension and the Python backend to talk to each other reliably.

Honest about what is unproven. Detection performance has not been formally measured, so this page lists no accuracy figures. The prototype misses content, and it flags material that is harmless.

What a next version would add. A future build would let people choose their engine: stay fully local, as it works today, or opt into a frontier model using retrieval-augmented generation, few-shot prompting and constrained outputs. The local option keeps browsing private; the frontier option would generalise to almost any social platform or streaming video. That choice has not been built yet.
